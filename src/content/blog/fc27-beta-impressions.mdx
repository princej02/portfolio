## Getting into the beta

I've been putting in hours on the FC 27 beta on PS5. As someone who's been playing FC for a long time, I went in with tempered expectations. Some of what I found surprised me, in a good way.

## Career Mode: same concept, way better execution

Career Mode is the same core concept as FC 26, but the UI is way better. It's cleaner, easier to navigate, and just feels more modern.

The transfer system is where the real improvements are:

- **Scout reports actually matter now.** A player's potential is no longer hidden after you've scouted them. You know exactly what you're investing in.
- **Transfer visibility.** You can now see which other clubs have made an offer for a player. It makes the whole market feel more intuitive and competitive.
- **Buying players isn't straightforward anymore.** You have to notify the selling club first. You can also declare interest in a player to gauge their mood towards a move before committing. It adds a layer of realism that Career Mode has needed for years.

## Gameplay: smooth, with skill moves taking centre stage

Gameplay felt smooth overall, with some small but noticeable differences from FC 26.

The headline change: **skill moves are a lot more effective**. I'd say they'll be the top thing in online games this year. If you can't do them, you'll be at a real disadvantage.

On the defensive side, **jockeying is the most important skill** to learn. You can't rely solely on the AI to defend for you anymore. Manual defending is back in a big way, and honestly, I'm here for it.

## The Grounds: the new mode that had me gripped

The Grounds is FC 27's big new addition. It reminds me a lot of old-school street football games: you build your own custom character and explore different locations and game types.

The modes on offer include Rush, Bucket Ball, 1v1 Classic, 2v2 Classic, and 3v3. It's excellent for playing with friends online, and there are different divisions to climb as well.

Progression is a big part of it:

- You choose an **archetype** and upgrade it as you play
- There are **40 levels**, each with a reward for levelling up
- You can **join a club or create one with your mates**. It works like real football, with transfer requests and club invites to manage

The best way I can describe it: a mini Ultimate Team, except you control your own player. This mode has the potential to win a lot of people over. It was fun, and it had me gripped.

## Early verdict

My verdict is 50/50.

Ultimate Team will be Ultimate Team. Nothing there dramatically shifts. Outside of the UI refresh and the smaller features added to Career Mode (both manager and player), the other modes don't look like they've changed all that much.

But the positives are real. The gameplay changes are quite noticeable, and The Grounds is going to be a fan favourite, especially for content creators, given how well the multiplayer works.

For me? I'd likely get the game. The Grounds alone has done enough to convince me.
